\paragraph{RGB and 3D policy representations.}
RGB imitation policies pair image encoders with diffusion or flow-style action
decoders, or with equivariant RGB encoders~\citep{chi2023diffusionpolicy,yan2025maniflow,klee2026raven,hu2025spherical}.
Explicit 3D policies instead use voxel, point-cloud, object-centric, ray-based,
or spherical-projected inputs~\citep{shridhar2023peract,goyal2023rvt,ze2024dp3,ke2024dda,yan2025maniflow,peri2024pcwm,wang2024equidiff,zhao2025hep,zhu2026equact,zhao2025gsl,klee2026raven,hu2025spherical},
which expose spatial structure but often require depth, calibration, or 3D-specific
encoders.  We keep the RGB-policy interface and learn camera geometry inside
the control objective.

\paragraph{Camera conditioning for RGB policies.}
The closest reference is Camera-Aware Multi-View Visuomotor
Policies~\citep{anonymous2026cameraaware}, which studies how RGB policies benefit
from explicit camera encodings under a controlled multi-view manipulation
protocol.  It compares learned camera IDs, calibrated per-pixel Plucker ray maps,
and attention-level pose encodings such as PRoPE/GTA-style relative
geometry~\citep{sitzmann2021lfn,li2025prope,miyato2024gta}.  It also evaluates a
decoupled calibration-free setting: a pose predictor is trained separately, then
its predictions are plugged into a frozen geometry-conditioned policy.  The key
distinction is that those camera-aware baselines either assume calibrated
geometry or learn geometry outside the policy objective.  Our policies use the
same Plucker-ray interface but build rays from poses predicted inside the
policy, so action and NVS losses shape the geometry used at inference.

\paragraph{View synthesis and camera-pose estimation.}
Ray- and pose-conditioned view-synthesis models learn renderable scene
representations~\citep{mildenhall2020nerf,sajjadi2022srt,jin2024lvsm,kim2026svsm,mitchel2026xfactor},
sometimes with cameras inferred or predicted from images~\citep{jiang2025rayzer,zhang2024camerasasrays,wang2024dust3r,leroy2024mast3r,wang2025vggt}.  We use
target-view reconstruction only as a training-time regularizer for control: the
NVS decoder is discarded at inference, while the scene tokens remain for action
prediction.
