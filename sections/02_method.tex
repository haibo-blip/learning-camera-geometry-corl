Our goal is to train an RGB policy that uses camera geometry without calibrated
scene-camera extrinsics at test time.  A pose predictor estimates each input
camera pose, differentiable ray construction converts the estimates into
per-pixel Plucker features, and the downstream policy is trained end-to-end
through those features.  \autoref{fig:pipeline} shows two variants: an
action-only policy trained with behavior cloning and pose supervision, and an
NVS-regularized policy that reconstructs target views from latent scene tokens
during training.  The NVS decoder is discarded at inference.

\subsection{Problem Setup}

At time \(t\), the policy observes \(N\) RGB views
\(\mathcal{I}_t=\{I_{t,i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\), proprioception \(q_t\), and task
condition \(c\), and predicts an action chunk \(A_t\).  Camera \(i\) has known
intrinsics \(K_i\) and an extrinsic transform \(T_{t,i}\in SE(3)\) expressed in
an world frame.  Training poses are episode-level labels: a
binary mask \(m_e\in\{0,1\}\) indicates whether episode \(e\) provides camera
poses, and the label ratio \(\rho\) is the fraction of labeled episodes.

\subsection{Semi-Supervised Camera-Pose Prediction}

Given the RGB views, a pose head \(h_\phi\) predicts a transform
\(\hat{T}_{t,i}=h_\phi(\mathcal{I}_t)_i\) for every input camera.  The
supervised pose loss is applied only to timesteps and views from labeled
episodes:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{pose}} =
\frac{1}{\max(1,\sum_{e,t,i}m_e)}
\sum_{e,t,i}m_e
\left[
w_R d_{SO(3)}(\hat{R}_{e,t,i},R_{e,t,i})
+ w_x \|\hat{x}_{e,t,i}-x_{e,t,i}\|_1
\right],
\end{equation}
where \(d_{SO(3)}\) is the geodesic rotation distance and \(x\) denotes
translation.  The loss is semi-supervised because it can be evaluated on any
chosen fraction of labeled episodes while the rest of the model still
receives action and, when enabled, NVS gradients.

The estimated pose is then turned into dense camera geometry.  For each pixel
coordinate \(u\), intrinsics \(K_i\) and pose \(\hat{T}_{t,i}\) define a ray
origin \(o_{t,i}(u)\) and direction \(d_{t,i}(u)\).  We encode these rays as a
six-channel Plucker ray map~\citep{plucker1865newgeometry,sitzmann2021lfn}
\begin{equation}
P_{t,i}(u)=\left[o_{t,i}(u)\times d_{t,i}(u),\ d_{t,i}(u)\right].
\end{equation}
We use the explicit Plucker form as a minimally intrusive geometry interface:
it augments RGB with pixel-aligned metric rays while leaving the encoder blocks
and action decoder unchanged.  Downstream action and NVS losses can still shape
the pose predictor through the ray-conditioned policy, so episodes without
camera-pose annotation need not be discarded.

\subsection{E2E Camera-Pose Action Policy}

The action-only variant tests whether action supervision and sparse,
episode-level pose labels are enough to learn useful camera geometry.  For each
view, we concatenate RGB and predicted ray features and tokenize the result with
an observation encoder:
\begin{equation}
z_t = f_\theta\left(\{I_{t,i}, P_{t,i}\}_{i=1}^{N}, q_t, c\right).
\end{equation}
This makes the variant a learned-pose counterpart of a Plucker-conditioned
policy rather than a PRoPE-style attention model: predicted geometry enters as
pixel-aligned Plucker maps before tokenization.
An action expert \(g_\psi\) predicts the action chunk with a conditional
flow-matching decoder; the standard flow-matching velocity objective
\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{act}}\) is given in
\autoref{sec:appendix-policy-architecture}.
The total objective for this variant is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{E2E-act}} =
\mathcal{L}_{\text{act}}+\lambda_{\text{pose}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{pose}}.
\end{equation}
At inference, no ground-truth camera pose is provided.  The model predicts
camera poses from RGB, builds ray features from the predictions, and samples an
action chunk from the action decoder.

\subsection{E2E Camera-Pose NVS Policy}

Action loss is a weak constraint on what the observation encoder must preserve. The encoder is only penalized for features that change the predicted action; layout in unviewed regions, occluded surfaces, and cross-view consistency can be discarded without cost. Under camera-distribution shift this is fragile: features dropped at training time because they did not change the immediate action may carry the only signal for a relation that now matters under a shifted view. We add novel-view synthesis as a \emph{renderability prior} on the observation representation: if a held-out target view can be reconstructed from the latent, the latent must preserve enough 3D layout to support an arbitrary camera query. This is a softer constraint than maintaining an explicit point cloud or voxel grid --- only renderability, not metric reconstruction --- but stronger than action loss alone. The NVS decoder is discarded at inference, so deployment cost is unchanged.

A separate encoder maps source views and their predicted ray features into latent scene tokens:
\begin{equation}
R_t = F_\theta\left(\{I_{t,i},P_{t,i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\right),
\qquad R_t\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times D}.
\end{equation}
This follows the set-latent scene representation used in view-synthesis
transformers~\citep{sajjadi2022srt,jin2024lvsm,jiang2025rayzer}: a fixed set of
learned scene tokens attends over the multi-view RGB--ray patches and the patch
tokens are then discarded, so the NVS branch decodes from a latent scene-token
bottleneck rather than the full source patch sequence (\(M{=}64\); encoder and
decoder sizes in \autoref{sec:appendix-nvs-training}).  The action expert uses
these tokens together with proprioception and task conditioning,
\(z_t^{\text{scene}} = \text{concat}(R_t, q_t, c)\), and is trained with the same
flow-matching action loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{act}}\) as the action-only
variant, now conditioned on \(z_t^{\text{scene}}\).

For the NVS objective, we randomly select a target view \(j\) and build its
query rays from the \emph{predicted} target pose \(\hat{T}_{t,j}\) --- the same
pose head applied to the target view --- together with its intrinsics.  Both
source- and target-view Plucker rays therefore come from predicted, not
ground-truth, poses, so the reconstruction objective requires no
camera-extrinsic labels: the target's RGB image is the only supervision it
needs, which is what lets episodes without pose labels still contribute NVS
gradients.
To reduce trivial copying, the target observation is stochastically masked from
the scene-token encoder input during NVS training.  When the mask is active, the
decoder must reconstruct the target view from the remaining views; otherwise the
target view remains available as an observed input.  The NVS decoder \(D_\omega\)
uses target-ray tokens as queries and attends to the scene tokens:
\begin{equation}
\hat{I}_{t,j} =
D_\omega\left(R_t, P_{t,j}\right).
\end{equation}
We train it with a pixel and perceptual reconstruction loss:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{nvs}} =
\|\hat{I}_{t,j}-I_{t,j}\|_2^2
+\beta\,\text{LPIPS}(\hat{I}_{t,j}, I_{t,j}).
\end{equation}
This objective forces the scene tokens to encode object layout, occlusion, and
camera geometry rather than only action-correlated image features.  The complete
training objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{NVS}} =
\mathcal{L}_{\text{act}}
+\lambda_{\text{pose}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{pose}}
+\lambda_{\text{nvs}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{nvs}}.
\end{equation}

At inference, the policy keeps the pose head, scene-token encoder, and action
expert, but discards the NVS decoder.  Additional architecture and NVS training
details are summarized in \autoref{sec:appendix-policy-architecture} and
\autoref{sec:appendix-nvs-training}.
