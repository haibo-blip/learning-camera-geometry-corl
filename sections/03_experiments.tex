\subsection{LIBERO-MV Spatial}

\paragraph{Goal and protocol.}
The LIBERO-MV Spatial experiment isolates how much camera-pose supervision is
needed for end-to-end camera-pose learning.  We follow the LIBERO-MV Spatial
protocol of Camera-Aware Multi-View Visuomotor Policies~\citep{anonymous2026cameraaware}:
policies receive two random surround views and are evaluated on all 10 Spatial
tasks with 50 rollouts each.  Resolution, action horizon, optimization, and
camera sampling details are in \autoref{sec:appendix-libero-protocol}.

\paragraph{Compared methods.}
For comparability, our implementation follows the reference policy stack: the
image backbone and action decoder are held fixed across methods, while the
geometry interface changes.  In the calibrated references, \texttt{ViT+CamID}
uses a learned camera identity with no metric geometry, \texttt{Plucker-GT}
concatenates calibrated Plucker ray maps to RGB, and \texttt{PRoPE-GT} injects
calibrated relative pose inside attention.  Our E2E camera-pose action policy
keeps the same Plucker-ray interface as \texttt{Plucker-GT}, but constructs rays
from RGB-predicted poses and trains the pose predictor jointly with action
prediction; it does not use the PRoPE attention injection.  Unlabeled episodes
contribute action loss but no pose loss.

\paragraph{Pose-label sweep.}
We vary the fraction of pose-labeled training episodes over
\(\{10,30,50,80,100\}\%\), keeping unlabeled episodes in the behavior-cloning
dataset.  \autoref{fig:libero} compares the calibrated references with the best
E2E run, while the full label-ratio curve is shown in
\autoref{fig:appendix-libero-label-sweep} in \autoref{sec:appendix-pose-sweep}.
The sweep shows that 10\% pose labels already exceed the calibrated Plucker
baseline (0.820 vs.\ 0.622), and the best E2E run matches the stronger
calibrated PRoPE reference.  Success remains high (0.78--0.87) despite large
differences in standalone pose error, suggesting that the action objective
shapes predicted geometry toward what the policy needs for control.

  \centering

  \caption{LIBERO-MV Spatial comparison between calibrated references from the
  Camera-Aware Multi-View Visuomotor Policies protocol and the best E2E
  predicted-Plucker action policy.}
  \label{fig:libero}

\paragraph{Coupled vs.\ decoupled geometry.}
A natural alternative is to train a pose head by supervised regression, then
plug its predictions into a frozen geometry-conditioned policy.  This decoupled
variant is a deployment-consistency test: the frozen policy is trained on
ground-truth rays but deployed with predicted rays.  Under the identical
protocol, it reaches \(0.660\) success using full pose supervision, whereas our
coupled policy reaches \(0.865\) with pose labels for only \(50\%\) of episodes.
The decoupled pose head has lower rotation error (\(0.33^\circ\) vs.\
\(2.42^\circ\)), so a low-error pose predictor is not automatically a drop-in
substitute for the geometry distribution used to train a policy.  The label
sweep provides a complementary within-coupled view: each run trains and
evaluates on its own predicted rays, and reducing rotation error from
\(7.6^\circ\) to \(0.16^\circ\) changes Spatial success by under five points
(\autoref{sec:appendix-pose-sweep}).

\subsection{MimicGen Camera-Shift Evaluation}

\paragraph{Protocol and baselines.}
MimicGen~\citep{mandlekar2023mimicgen} provides stronger episode-to-episode
variation than LIBERO-MV.  We evaluate four tasks --- Stack, Stack Three, Coffee,
and Threading --- under the training \texttt{train\_wide} camera distribution and
a held-out \texttt{eval\_tight} distribution illustrated in \autoref{fig:overview}.
Each camera setting uses 40 rollouts per evaluation checkpoint, and checkpoint
selection is based on validation rollout success.  We compare to Diffusion
Policy~\citep{chi2023diffusionpolicy} as an image baseline and
ManiFlow~\citep{yan2025maniflow} as a point-cloud baseline.  ManiFlow uses
ground-truth camera extrinsics to construct its point-cloud input; split,
camera, and hyperparameter details are in \autoref{sec:appendix-mimicgen-protocol}, and
checkpoint selection is described in
\autoref{sec:appendix-checkpoint-selection}.

\paragraph{Results.}
\autoref{fig:mimicgen-results} reports episode-level success.  The NVS policy
improves over the E2E camera-pose action policy by 13.1 points on train-camera
evaluation and 23.1 points under tight-camera evaluation.  On Stack, it matches
the action-only variant on train-camera evaluation at 97.5\% and improves
tight-camera success from 2.5\% to 42.5\%.  Relative to ManiFlow, the comparison
is mixed: averaged over four tasks, our NVS policy leads on train-camera success
(46.9\% vs.\ 34.4\%) but trails on tight-camera success (23.8\% vs.\ 31.9\%).
The result is therefore an RGB-only policy comparison with competitive
camera-shift behavior, not a claim that RGB policies replace depth-based
point-cloud methods in every shifted-camera regime.

\paragraph{Ablations.}
Stack Three ablations support both design choices
(\autoref{fig:mimicgen-ablations}).  Removing end-to-end camera-pose prediction
and NVS gives 17.5\% train-camera success and 0\% tight-camera success; adding
E2E pose prediction lifts train-camera success to 30\%, and adding NVS reaches
55\% train / 25\% tight.  Removing the scene-token bottleneck keeps train-camera
success at 55\% but drops tight-camera success from 25\% to 7.5\%, indicating
that the bottleneck matters most under camera shift.

\subsection{Real-World Tabletop Evaluation}

\paragraph{Protocol and supervision budget.}
We evaluate on a single-arm Trossen WidowX AI with one wrist RealSense D405 and
two scene-side RealSense D455F cameras.  The comparison is matched in protocol:
our policy and both references use the same platform, camera hardware, rubrics,
trial counts, and success definitions, and differ only in how camera geometry is
obtained. During data
collection their calibrated extrinsics vary across episode.  The Plucker-GT and Plucker-RayZer numbers
are the calibrated and decoupled baselines evaluated under this protocol on the
same hardware~\citep{anonymous2026cameraaware}; baseline definitions, scoring,
and dataset details are in \autoref{sec:appendix-realworld-protocol}.  Our E2E
camera-pose NVS policy uses pose labels for 50\% of training episodes and no
calibration at inference.

\paragraph{Results.}
\autoref{tab:realworld} compares our coupled NVS policy against a calibrated
Plucker-GT reference and a decoupled Plucker-RayZer baseline.  Cup Stacking and
Stand-up Cup use 20 trials each and Blocks-in-Bowl uses 10.  Against the
decoupled calibration-free baseline, our policy is at least as strong on every
task and wins decisively on Stand-up Cup (75 vs.\ 55) and Blocks-in-Bowl
(100 vs.\ 30).  It also exceeds the calibrated Plucker-GT reference in aggregate
(86.7 vs.\ 70.0 mean success), although the gain is driven mainly by
Blocks-in-Bowl and the calibrated reference remains saturated on Cup Stacking.
