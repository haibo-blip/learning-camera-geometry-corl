\subsection{LIBERO-MV Spatial}

The LIBERO-MV Spatial experiment isolates end-to-end camera-pose learning.  We
vary the fraction of training episodes with ground-truth camera-pose labels and
compare against calibrated geometry-aware references.  Fig.~\ref{fig:libero}
shows two trends.  First, a small amount of pose supervision is already useful:
the 10\% setting reaches a success rate close to the calibrated Plucker
baseline.  Second, the best current 50\% setting exceeds the calibrated PRoPE
reference in this sweep, suggesting that action loss can shape the predicted
geometry toward what the policy needs for control.

\subsection{Real-World Tabletop Evaluation}

We are also beginning a real-world tabletop evaluation following the
camera-conditioned tabletop setting of \citet{jiang2025knowyourcamera}.  For a
first comparison, Fig.~\ref{fig:realworld-blocks} uses only their calibrated
Plucker-GT result on Blocks-in-Bowl and compares it with our current E2E
camera-pose NVS policy.  Camera Rays reports 50\% full-task success and 86\%
rubric-weighted task progress over 10 trials for Plucker-GT on this task.  Our
current Blocks-in-Bowl run has 100\% full-task success on the completed trials,
but the final trial denominator is still pending from the run log; we therefore
mark the result as preliminary.

\subsection{Planned Baselines and Ablations}

The main completed baseline set compares image Diffusion Policy, point-cloud
ManiFlow, the E2E camera-pose action policy, and the E2E camera-pose NVS policy.
The next ablations should separate the effects of pose-label ratio, action-loss
end-to-end training, NVS regularization, target-view dropout, and the
scene-token bottleneck.
