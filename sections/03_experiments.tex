\subsection{LIBERO-MV Spatial}

\textbf{Setup.} We compare predicted Plucker rays, trained jointly with the policy, against three calibrated geometry references and a decoupled alternative in which a pose head is trained by supervised regression and plugged into a frozen policy trained on calibrated rays. We follow the LIBERO-MV Spatial protocol of Camera-Aware Multi-View Visuomotor Policies~\citep{anonymous2026cameraaware}: two random surround views per timestep, 10 Spatial tasks, 50 rollouts each; resolution, action horizon, optimization, and camera sampling are in \autoref{sec:appendix-libero-protocol}. Compared methods share the same image backbone and action decoder and differ only in the geometry interface. The calibrated references are \texttt{ViT+CamID} (learned camera ID, no metric geometry), \texttt{Plucker-GT} (calibrated Plucker rays concatenated to RGB), and \texttt{PRoPE-GT} (calibrated relative pose injected inside attention). Our E2E camera-pose action policy keeps the \texttt{Plucker-GT} interface but constructs rays from RGB-predicted poses and trains the pose predictor jointly with action prediction; unlabeled episodes contribute action loss but no pose loss. We vary the fraction of pose-labeled episodes over \(\{10, 30, 50, 80, 100\}\%\), keeping unlabeled episodes in the behavior-cloning dataset.

\textbf{Result.} \autoref{tab:libero} compares the calibrated references with
the best E2E run, and the full label-ratio curve is shown in
\autoref{fig:appendix-libero-label-sweep} in \autoref{sec:appendix-pose-sweep}.
The sweep shows that 10\% pose labels already exceed the calibrated Plucker
baseline (0.820 vs.\ 0.622), and the best E2E run matches the stronger
calibrated PRoPE reference. Across the sweep, success stays high (0.78--0.87)
despite a 48\(\times\) swing in rotation error (from \(7.6^\circ\) at 10\% to
\(0.16^\circ\) at 100\%, \autoref{sec:appendix-pose-sweep}), so within coupled
training the action objective shapes predicted geometry toward what the policy
needs for control rather than metric calibration alone. The decoupled baseline
reaches \(0.660\) success with full pose supervision and a 7\(\times\) more
accurate pose head (\(0.33^\circ\) rotation error vs.\ our \(2.42^\circ\) at
50\% labels), trailing the coupled policy by over 20 success points: lower pose
error is not sufficient when the policy was trained on calibrated rays and
deployed on predicted ones. Coupled training, not pose accuracy alone, is what
makes predicted geometry useful for control.

\subsection{MimicGen Camera-Shift Evaluation}

\textbf{Setup.} MimicGen~\citep{mandlekar2023mimicgen} introduces stronger episode-to-episode camera variation than LIBERO-MV; we use it to evaluate whether NVS regularization translates the renderability prior into camera-shift robustness. We evaluate four tasks --- Stack, Stack Three, Coffee, and Threading --- under the training \texttt{train\_wide} distribution and a held-out \texttt{eval\_tight} distribution illustrated in \autoref{fig:overview}, with 40 rollouts per camera setting per checkpoint. We compare Diffusion Policy~\citep{chi2023diffusionpolicy} (image baseline), ManiFlow~\citep{yan2025maniflow} (point-cloud baseline, using ground-truth camera extrinsics to construct its point-cloud input), and our two E2E camera-pose variants (action-only and NVS-regularized) at 50\% pose labels. Split, camera, hyperparameter, and checkpoint-selection details are in \autoref{sec:appendix-mimicgen-protocol} and \autoref{sec:appendix-checkpoint-selection}.

\textbf{Result.} \autoref{fig:mimicgen-results} reports episode-level success. Averaged over four tasks, the NVS policy improves over the action-only camera-pose policy by 13.1 points on train-camera evaluation and 23.1 points under tight-camera evaluation; on Stack it matches the action-only variant at 97.5\% train-camera while lifting tight-camera success from 2.5\% to 42.5\%. The renderability prior thus pays off most under camera shift, consistent with the design hypothesis that action loss alone underconstrains the observation encoder. A tight-camera failure analysis in \autoref{sec:appendix-tight-failure} shows that the success drop is accompanied by larger predicted-pose errors, while NVS reduces this error relative to the action-only policy on Coffee D2. Against ManiFlow the comparison is mixed: our NVS policy leads on train-camera success (46.9\% vs.\ 34.4\%) but trails under tight-camera shift (23.8\% vs.\ 31.9\%).

\textbf{Ablation.} The Stack Three ablation (\autoref{fig:mimicgen-ablations}) supports both design choices. Removing end-to-end camera-pose prediction and NVS gives 17.5\% train-camera success and 0\% tight-camera success; adding E2E pose prediction lifts train-camera success to 30\%; adding NVS on top reaches 55\% train / 25\% tight. Removing the scene-token bottleneck while keeping the NVS loss leaves train-camera success unchanged at 55\% but drops tight-camera success from 25\% to 7.5\%, indicating that the bottleneck is where the renderability prior actually shapes the representation under shift.

\subsection{Real-World Tabletop Evaluation}

\textbf{Setup.} We evaluate the coupled NVS policy on a real-robot tabletop where the scene cameras are physically displaced between trials and no calibrated extrinsics are provided at inference; the pose head must infer geometry from RGB alone. The platform is a single-arm Trossen WidowX AI with one wrist RealSense D405 and two scene-side RealSense D455F cameras; calibrated extrinsics vary across episodes during data collection. The matched-protocol comparison uses the same platform, hardware, rubrics, trial counts, and success definitions; methods differ only in how camera geometry is obtained. Plucker-GT (calibrated, full pose labels) and Plucker-RayZer (decoupled, calibration-free) numbers are evaluated under this protocol on the same hardware~\citep{anonymous2026cameraaware}. Our E2E camera-pose NVS policy uses pose labels for 50\% of training episodes and no calibration at inference. Cup Stacking and Stand-up Cup use 20 trials each; Blocks-in-Bowl uses 10. Baseline definitions, scoring, and dataset details are in \autoref{sec:appendix-realworld-protocol}.

\textbf{Result.} \autoref{tab:realworld} compares the three methods. Against the decoupled calibration-free baseline, our coupled NVS policy is at least as strong on every task and wins decisively on Stand-up Cup (75 vs.\ 55) and Blocks-in-Bowl (100 vs.\ 30), echoing the LIBERO finding that joint training beats a separately-trained pose head plugged in at inference. Against the calibrated Plucker-GT upper bound, the aggregate is in our favor (86.7 vs.\ 70.0 mean success), but the comparison is task-dependent: Cup Stacking remains saturated for the calibrated reference (100 vs.\ 85), while the gain is concentrated on Blocks-in-Bowl, which uses the smallest trial count.
