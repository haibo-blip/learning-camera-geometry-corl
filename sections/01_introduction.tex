RGB visuomotor policies can map image observations and proprioception to action
chunks with diffusion, flow-style, or equivariant decoders~\citep{chi2023diffusionpolicy,yan2025maniflow,klee2026raven,hu2025spherical},
but they usually leave camera geometry implicit.  As cameras move, the same
object relation appears at different image locations and occlusion or contact
cues may be visible only from certain views.  Image-only policies can therefore
learn view-specific shortcuts that weaken under camera shift.

Explicit geometry helps, but it raises the data requirement.  Calibrated
camera-aware RGB policies expose Plucker rays or pose encodings to the
encoder~\citep{anonymous2026cameraaware}; point-cloud, voxel, and
object-centric 3D policies expose spatial structure through depth, calibration,
or structured 3D representations~\citep{shridhar2023peract,goyal2023rvt,ze2024dp3,ke2024dda,yan2025maniflow,peri2024pcwm,zhu2026equact,zhao2025gsl}.  Demonstrations
without extrinsics cannot directly supply those geometric features, and many 3D
pipelines additionally require depth and modality-specific encoders.

We ask whether camera geometry can be learned inside the control objective
instead of provided as fixed metadata.  A pose predictor estimates camera
extrinsics from RGB, differentiable ray construction converts the predictions
into per-pixel Plucker features, and the entire policy is trained end-to-end
from RGB to actions.  Only a subset of training episodes needs pose labels; the
remaining episodes can still shape camera-pose learning through the action loss.
In the NVS variant, target-view queries are also built from predicted poses, so
unlabeled episodes additionally provide view-synthesis supervision through their
RGB targets.  Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarizes the action-only and
NVS-regularized variants, while Fig.~\ref{fig:overview} illustrates the training
signals.

We evaluate on LIBERO-MV Spatial~\citep{liu2023libero}, MimicGen~\citep{mandlekar2023mimicgen},
and real-world tabletop tasks.  On LIBERO-MV, a coupled policy with
pose labels on only half of the episodes outperforms a fully-supervised
decoupled pose-then-policy baseline by over 20\%; within coupled training,
a \(48\times\) rotation-error reduction changes success by under 5\%.
On MimicGen, NVS improves over the action-only variant by 13.1\% on
train-camera evaluation and 23.1\% under tight-camera evaluation.  On the
real robot, the NVS policy outperforms a decoupled calibration-free baseline and
exceeds the calibrated Plucker-GT aggregate while using no calibration at
inference.

The contribution is an end-to-end formulation for semi-supervised camera
geometry learning, with action-only and scene-token NVS variants, showing that
task-useful geometry can be learned inside the policy objective while retaining
training signal from episodes without camera extrinsics.
