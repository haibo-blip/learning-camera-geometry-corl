\paragraph{Shared policy architecture.}
Unless otherwise stated, the RGB observation encoder uses ViT-S scale
components with hidden size 384, 6 transformer layers, 6 attention heads, and
patch size 16.  The action expert is a flow-matching DiT with hidden size 384,
8 layers, and 6 attention heads.  Policies predict 32-step action chunks and
execute actions with an 8-step replanning interval in simulation.  Actions are
end-effector translation, 6D rotation, and gripper command.

\paragraph{Flow-matching action objective.}
With \(x_1=A_t\) the demonstration action chunk, \(x_0\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)\), and
\(\tau\sim U[0,1]\), the action expert \(g_\psi\) regresses the conditional
flow-matching velocity field along the interpolation
\(x_\tau = (1-(1-\sigma_{\min})\tau)x_0+\tau x_1\), with target
\(u_\tau = x_1-(1-\sigma_{\min})x_0\):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{act}} =
\mathbb{E}_{x_0,\tau}\left[\|g_\psi(x_\tau,\tau,z_t)-u_\tau\|_2^2\right],
\end{equation}
where \(z_t\) is the (ray-conditioned or scene-token) observation embedding.

\paragraph{Pose and ray construction.}
All proposed variants assume known camera intrinsics, but do not use calibrated scene-camera extrinsics at inference.
The pose head predicts the scene-camera extrinsics in the world
frame from RGB observations.  Predicted extrinsics and known intrinsics are then
converted into dense Plucker ray maps for the policy input.  In the NVS variant,
both source-view rays and target-view query rays are constructed from these
predicted poses; ground-truth extrinsics are never fed to the policy or NVS
decoder.  They are used only to compute \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{pose}}\) on the
episode subset selected for pose supervision.  Unlabeled episodes therefore
still contribute action loss, and in the NVS variant they also contribute
view-synthesis loss through their RGB target images.  The pose predictor uses
hidden size 384, 6 transformer layers, 6 attention heads, and patch size 16.
The reported runs use \(w_R=1.0\), \(w_x=0.5\), and
\(\lambda_{\text{pose}}=1.0\).

\paragraph{Auxiliary losses and NVS training.}
The action-only policy optimizes \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{act}}+
\lambda_{\text{pose}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{pose}}\).  The NVS policy adds
\(\lambda_{\text{nvs}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{nvs}}\) with
\(\lambda_{\text{nvs}}=1.0\).  The scene-token encoder uses hidden size 384, 12
transformer layers, 6 attention heads, patch size 8, and 64 active scene tokens.
The encoder appends learned active scene tokens to the multi-view patch sequence;
after self-attention, patch tokens are discarded and only the active scene tokens
are retained.  The NVS decoder uses hidden size 384, 8 transformer layers, and 6
attention heads.  \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{nvs}}\) combines pixel MSE with VGG-LPIPS at
\(\beta=0.5\).  For each NVS training example, one available view is sampled as
the target.  Its RGB image is the reconstruction target, while its predicted ray
map is used as the decoder query.  The target observation is masked from the
scene-token encoder input with probability \(p_{\text{drop}}=0.6\); when the mask
is active, the scene tokens must reconstruct the target view from the remaining
source observations.
This dropout is used only for the NVS branch and is not used by the
action-only E2E policy.  Flow-matching inference uses \(\sigma_{\min}=0\) and
10 denoising steps.

\paragraph{LIBERO-MV Spatial.}
LIBERO-MV experiments follow the setup of Camera-Aware Multi-View Visuomotor
Policies~\citep{anonymous2026cameraaware}: two random surround views
(\texttt{surround2}), no wrist view, \(128{\times}128\) RGB inputs, 100k
training steps, batch size 256, AdamW with learning rate \(10^{-4}\), cosine
schedule, 1k warmup steps, bf16 mixed precision, and EMA 0.9999.  Evaluation
uses 10 tasks and 50 rollouts per task.

\paragraph{MimicGen.}
MimicGen policies use 12 random surround cameras per episode and a 5\% demo
holdout.  Rollout evaluation is run every 50k optimization steps under
both \texttt{train\_wide} and \texttt{eval\_tight}, with 40 rollouts for each
camera setting.  The E2E camera-pose policies use 50\% episode-level pose
labels in the reported runs.  The NVS variant uses 64 active scene tokens and
target-observation dropout probability 0.6 during NVS training.  Diffusion
Policy and ManiFlow use their original training recipes as closely as possible;
ManiFlow uses 1024 point-cloud points.

\paragraph{Checkpoint selection and reporting.}
For MimicGen, each saved evaluation point runs rollouts on the training camera
distribution and on the tighter held-out camera distribution.  The paper reports
the checkpoint with the highest validation rollout success over the full
training run's scheduled validation evaluations.  Reported train-camera and
tight-camera success rates come from the same selected checkpoint, rather than
from separately selecting a best checkpoint for each camera setting.

\paragraph{Real-World Tabletop.}
The real-world training set contains \(3{,}171\) teleoperated episodes across
five household task prompts; the table in the main text reports the three tasks
covered by the matched evaluation protocol.  The E2E camera-pose NVS policy uses
50\% episode-level pose labels and no calibration at inference.  Plucker-GT uses
the same policy structure as our E2E camera-pose action policy, but removes the
camera-pose predictor and constructs Plucker rays from ground-truth camera
poses.  Plucker-RayZer uses the same action-only policy structure, but trains
the camera-pose predictor separately from the action expert and uses 100\% of
the pose-labeled data for that pose training.  All conditions use the same robot
platform, camera hardware, rubrics, and trial counts; the Plucker-GT and
Plucker-RayZer reference numbers are the calibrated and decoupled baselines
evaluated under the same protocol on the same
hardware~\citep{anonymous2026cameraaware}.  The scene-camera extrinsics are
stored with the data and vary across collection episodes; in the
calibration-free rollout protocol, the scene cameras are also physically moved
between trials before poses are re-predicted.  Actions are predicted in the
absolute end-effector frame (position, 6D rotation, continuous gripper) as
1-second chunks at 50 Hz.  The platform is a Trossen WidowX AI with one wrist
Intel RealSense D405 camera and two scene-side RealSense D455F cameras.  Each
cell reports full-task success and rubric-weighted task progress.  Cup Stacking
and Stand-up Cup use 20 trials each (rubric max 2 points per trial);
Blocks-in-Bowl uses 10 trials under a 4-minute wall-clock cap, with one point
per successful grasp and one per successful placement across four blocks (eight
points per trial; full success requires all four blocks placed).
