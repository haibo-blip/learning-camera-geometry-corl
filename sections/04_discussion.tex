The results suggest that learned camera geometry should not be evaluated only as metric calibration.  A pose predictor optimized jointly with action can learn a geometry representation that is useful for the downstream policy, even when it is not a drop-in replacement for calibrated extrinsics in every setting.  This helps explain why partial pose supervision can outperform calibrated geometry baselines in the LIBERO-MV Spatial sweep. Concretely, a decoupled pose head that is roughly 7$\times$ more accurate in rotation than our 50\% coupled run (0.33$^\circ$ vs.\ 2.42$^\circ$) still trails it by over 20 points in task success (0.660 vs.\ 0.865), and across the label sweep a 48$\times$ reduction in rotation error moves success by under five points: metric pose error and control success do not move together.

Novel-view synthesis adds a stronger pressure on the observation representation.  The decoder cannot reconstruct a held-out view from a target camera query unless the scene tokens preserve object state, occlusion, and cross-view geometry.  This does not require the latent representation to be an explicit point cloud or voxel map; it only requires the representation to be renderable enough to support the auxiliary task.  In the MimicGen results, the NVS variant improves tight-camera success over the action-only variant on each evaluated task.

The comparison with explicit 3D methods should still be read with a clear boundary.  ManiFlow remains strong on some tasks, especially Stack tight-camera evaluation, so the claim is not that renderable latent representations uniformly dominate point clouds.  The sharper advantage is data use: calibrated ray and point-cloud pipelines need known geometry, depth, or both to construct their explicit features, whereas the proposed policies can keep training from episodes without extrinsic labels through action loss and, for the NVS variant, target-view reconstruction.
