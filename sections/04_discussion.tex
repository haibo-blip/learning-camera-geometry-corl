The results suggest that learned camera geometry should be evaluated in the policy context, not only as standalone calibration.  The LIBERO-MV decoupled comparison is a deployment-consistency test: a policy trained on ground-truth geometry can be sensitive to replacing that geometry with pose-head predictions, even when those predictions are metrically accurate.  Concretely, a decoupled pose head that is roughly 7$\times$ more accurate in rotation than our 50\% coupled run (0.33$^\circ$ vs.\ 2.42$^\circ$) still trails it by over 20 points in task success (0.660 vs.\ 0.865).  The label sweep gives a complementary within-coupled result: every coupled run trains and evaluates on its own predicted geometry, yet a 48$\times$ reduction in rotation error moves success by under five points.  These observations motivate end-to-end training, where the policy is optimized on the predicted geometry it will use at inference.

Novel-view synthesis adds a stronger pressure on the observation representation.  When the target observation is masked, the decoder cannot reconstruct the target view from a camera query unless the scene tokens preserve object state, occlusion, and cross-view geometry.  This does not require the latent representation to be an explicit point cloud or voxel map; it only requires the representation to be renderable enough to support the auxiliary task.  In the MimicGen results, the NVS variant improves tight-camera success over the action-only variant on each evaluated task.

The comparison with explicit 3D methods should still be read with a clear boundary.  Averaged over the four MimicGen tasks, the point-cloud ManiFlow baseline in fact attains higher tight-camera success than our NVS policy (31.9\% vs.\ 23.8\%).  This bounds the interpretation: the NVS policy is an RGB-only method that leads on train-camera evaluation and remains competitive under tight-camera shift, while point-cloud methods can still retain an advantage in some shifted-camera regimes.  The sharper advantage is data use: calibrated ray and point-cloud pipelines need known geometry, depth, or both to construct their explicit features, whereas the proposed policies can keep training from episodes without extrinsic labels through action loss and, for the NVS variant, target-view reconstruction.
