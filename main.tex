\begin{abstract}
RGB robot policies often treat camera placement as fixed context, even though the camera pose determines which spatial cues are visible and how pixels correspond across views. Geometry-aware policies can condition on calibrated poses, rays, depth, or point clouds, but those signals are typically supplied outside the policy objective and are unavailable for episodes without camera extrinsics. This paper asks whether camera geometry can instead be learned inside visuomotor training. We study two end-to-end camera-aware imitation learning variants: an action policy that predicts camera poses and conditions its observation encoder on pose/ray tokens, and an NVS-regularized policy that adds target-view reconstruction over latent scene tokens. The pose predictor is anchored by a semi-supervised auxiliary loss on a subset of pose-labeled episodes, while all episodes can still train the policy through action loss and, in the NVS variant, view-synthesis loss.

On LIBERO-MV Spatial, partial pose supervision is sufficient for strong control: 10\% pose labels approaches the calibrated Plucker baseline, and the best 50\% setting exceeds calibrated geometry-aware baselines. On preliminary MimicGen evaluations across four tasks, NVS regularization improves over the action-only camera-aware policy by 13.1 percentage points on train-camera evaluation and 23.1 points under tight-camera evaluation. These results suggest that camera geometry is most useful when optimized jointly with control, and that view-synthesis supervision can turn unlabeled camera episodes into useful representation-learning signal without requiring an explicit 3D reconstruction pipeline.
\end{abstract}
