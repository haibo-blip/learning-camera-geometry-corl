\begin{abstract}
Visuomotor policies often rely on calibrated camera geometry when cameras move beyond a fixed training setup, while image-only policies avoid calibration but may learn view-specific shortcuts that can fail under camera shift. Explicit 3D and point-cloud pipelines provide geometric structure, but require depth, calibration, and separate reconstruction stages. This paper asks whether camera geometry can instead be learned inside the control objective. We study two end-to-end camera-aware imitation learning variants: an action policy that predicts camera poses and conditions its observation encoder on pose/ray tokens, and an NVS-regularized policy that adds a novel-view decoder over register-style observation tokens. The pose predictor is trained with a semi-supervised auxiliary loss using only a subset of pose labels, while action loss and, in the NVS variant, held-out view reconstruction jointly shape the learned geometry.

On LIBERO-MV Spatial, partial pose supervision is sufficient for strong control: 10\% pose labels approaches calibrated PRoPE performance, and the best 50\% setting exceeds calibrated geometry-aware baselines. On preliminary MimicGen camera-shift evaluations across four tasks, NVS regularization improves tight-camera success over the action-only camera-aware policy on the tasks where both variants are available. These results suggest that camera geometry is most useful when optimized jointly with control, and that view-synthesis supervision can further regularize observation encoders without requiring an explicit 3D reconstruction pipeline.
\end{abstract}
