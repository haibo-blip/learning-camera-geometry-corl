\begin{abstract}
Camera placement is a hidden variable in RGB-based visuomotor learning: it determines which spatial cues are visible to the policy, yet it is often either ignored by image-only models or supplied as fixed calibration to geometry-aware models. Image-only policies avoid calibration but may learn view-specific shortcuts, while explicit 3D and point-cloud pipelines provide geometric structure at the cost of depth, calibration, or separate reconstruction stages. This paper asks whether camera geometry can instead be learned inside the control objective. We study two end-to-end camera-aware imitation learning variants: an action policy that predicts camera poses and conditions its observation encoder on pose/ray tokens, and an NVS-regularized policy that adds target-view reconstruction over latent scene tokens. The pose predictor is trained with a semi-supervised auxiliary loss using only a subset of pose labels, while action loss and, in the NVS variant, held-out view synthesis jointly shape the learned geometry.

On LIBERO-MV Spatial, partial pose supervision is sufficient for strong control: 10\% pose labels approaches calibrated PRoPE performance, and the best 50\% setting exceeds calibrated geometry-aware baselines. On preliminary MimicGen evaluations across four tasks, NVS regularization improves over the action-only camera-aware policy under both train-camera and tight-camera evaluation on the tasks where both variants are available. These results suggest that camera geometry is most useful when optimized jointly with control, and that view-synthesis supervision can regularize observation encoders without requiring an explicit 3D reconstruction pipeline.
\end{abstract}
