\begin{abstract}
RGB robot policies often treat camera placement as fixed context, even though the camera pose determines which spatial cues are visible and how pixels correspond across views. Geometry-aware policies can condition on calibrated poses, rays, depth, or point clouds, but those signals are typically supplied outside the policy objective and are unavailable for episodes without camera extrinsics. This paper asks whether camera geometry can instead be learned inside visuomotor training. We study two end-to-end camera-aware imitation learning variants: an action policy that predicts camera poses and conditions its observation encoder on predicted ray features, and an NVS-regularized policy that adds target-view reconstruction over latent scene tokens. A semi-supervised pose loss is applied only to pose-labeled episodes, while all episodes can still train the policy through action loss and, in the NVS variant, view-synthesis loss.

On LIBERO-MV Spatial, partial pose supervision is sufficient for strong control: 10\% pose labels approaches the calibrated Plucker baseline, and the best 50\% setting exceeds calibrated geometry-aware baselines. On MimicGen evaluations across four tasks, NVS regularization improves over the action-only camera-aware policy by 13.1 percentage points on train-camera evaluation and 23.1 points under tight-camera evaluation. On a real-world tabletop suite evaluated under a protocol matched to a calibrated Plucker-GT reference, the NVS policy matches or exceeds that reference (e.g., 100\% vs.\ 50\% full-task success on Blocks-in-Bowl) while using camera-pose labels for only half of the training episodes and no calibration at inference. These results suggest that camera geometry is most useful when optimized jointly with control, and that view-synthesis supervision can turn episodes without camera-extrinsic labels into useful representation-learning signal.
\end{abstract}
