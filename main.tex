\begin{abstract}
RGB robot policies often leave camera geometry implicit, while calibrated ray,
depth, and point-cloud policies require geometry that is unavailable for many
demonstrations.  We study end-to-end camera-geometry learning for imitation:
a pose head predicts camera extrinsics from RGB, differentiable Plucker ray
construction feeds an action policy, and an optional scene-token NVS branch
reconstructs target views from predicted-pose queries.  Ground-truth extrinsics
supervise only a subset pose loss; unlabeled episodes still train through action
loss and, for the NVS variant, view-synthesis loss.  On LIBERO-MV Spatial, using
pose labels on 50\% of episodes yields 0.865 success, outperforming a fully
supervised decoupled pose-then-policy baseline at 0.660.  Across the coupled
label sweep, a 48$\times$ reduction in rotation error changes success by under
five points.  On four MimicGen tasks, NVS improves over the action-only variant
by 13.1 train-camera points and 23.1 tight-camera points.  On matched real-world
tabletop tasks, the NVS policy outperforms a decoupled calibration-free baseline
and exceeds the calibrated Plucker-GT aggregate while using pose labels for only
half of training episodes and no calibration at inference.
\end{abstract}
